# Weekly trends — 2026-11-15

## AI

Coverage has shifted from scattered security/policy stories in late October to a dominant narrative of financial and governance upheaval by mid-November, with Anthropic's trillion-dollar IPO and Microsoft's mass layoffs recurring as proof of AI's real economic bite. A steady undercurrent of skepticism about AI's actual business value has persisted throughout, alongside mounting evidence of infrastructure strain (energy, water, data centers) and community backlash, which boiled over after the US midterms delivered a political rebuke to Big Tech. The Jensen Huang/Nvidia scandal has recently eclipsed almost everything else, reshaping discussions of chip policy, succession, and US-China tech rivalry. Accountability stories (lawsuits, pirated training data, agent safety) have also grown more frequent, suggesting the industry's 'move fast' phase is colliding with legal and ethical reckoning.

## Tech & engineering

Security and infrastructure fragility have been the dominant, unbroken theme across the whole period — certificate authority compromises, SSH and firmware flaws, crypto heists, and botnets recur almost weekly. Recently this has escalated from software vulnerabilities toward physical and geopolitical infrastructure risk: subsea cable threats, a Jane Street datacenter near-miss, a major Microsoft outage hitting hospitals and governments, and hardware supply strain from Taiwan labor strikes and missing Nvidia shipments. A secondary, persistent thread is platform power and openness (Android botnets, GitHub alternatives, Secure Boot gaps), showing growing unease about concentration of control. AI-adjacent tooling (xAI's Grok leak, AI-built app security failures) is a newer addition, suggesting the AI boom is now generating its own distinct engineering risk category.

## War, conflict & humanity

Gaza's humanitarian crisis has been the most consistent throughline, worsening steadily from aid cuts in October to a harsh winter with inadequate shelter by mid-November, even as a fragile ceasefire nominally holds. Sudan's war and DR Congo's Ebola outbreak have also been persistent, escalating backdrops — drone strikes intensifying in Sudan, Ebola response hampered by conflict in Congo — that rarely make top headlines despite their scale. Ukraine coverage has oscillated between diplomatic progress (ceasefires, prisoner exchanges, peace talks) and continued deadly strikes on civilians and infrastructure, reflecting the war's unresolved nature. Newer or resurfacing threads include rising Iran-Israel-US tensions and a string of under-covered crises (Yemen, Lebanon, Haiti, migrant boat disasters) that recur briefly before fading from view.

## Perspective

This section has consistently centered on lives and communities underrepresented in mainstream coverage — Indigenous land defenders, African narratives resisting flattening stereotypes, neurodivergent voices, and workers behind global supply chains (AI data labeling, recycling, chip manufacturing). A recurring aim is bridging divides, whether political, cultural, or experiential, with recent entries on former extremists, post-election unity, and cross-political dialogue. Coverage became sparser in early-to-mid November (several days with only one story), suggesting either fewer strong picks or a narrower selection. Newer entries lean into humanitarian intersections — childbirth under climate stress, confinement and displacement — echoing themes from the War & Conflict section but through a more individual, reflective lens.

## Economy

UK fiscal policy (Budget decisions, tax thresholds, wage floors) dominated late October, consistently framed around its squeeze on household finances. From late October onward, AI valuation jitters became a major economic throughline, with stock selloffs, Anthropic's IPO, and eventually the Nvidia/Jensen Huang scandal repeatedly rattling markets and tech-heavy indices. Cost-of-living pressure has been the one truly persistent theme throughout the entire period — inflation, energy bills, retail struggles — surfacing in nearly every entry regardless of what else dominates the day. Most recently, trade security and chip supply chains have emerged as a new focal point, tying economic policy more tightly to geopolitical risk.
